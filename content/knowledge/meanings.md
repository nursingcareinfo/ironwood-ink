# Meanings and Symbolism

The semantic layer of the atlas. Motifs carry meaning; combinations of motifs
compose personal narratives.

## Perseverance
Embodied by the koi swimming upstream. The central Japanese virtue of enduring
hardship without giving up.

## Transformation
The dragon koi — a carp that climbed the waterfall and became a dragon. Also the
serpent shedding its skin. Change achieved through struggle.

## Protection
The tiger and the dragon both guard against evil. Placement often chosen near
the heart or on the back (watching your back).

## Love and devotion
The rose (love with its thorns), the heart (courageous love), the swallow
(loyalty and return). Traditional vocabulary of commitment.

## Memento mori
The skull — an honest acceptance of mortality. The blackwork philosophy: ink is
permanent, so is death; wear both with composure.

## Heritage
Tribal / Polynesian patterns encode genealogy and social standing. Worn as
identity, designed with cultural respect.

## Healing
The serpent's shedding, the chrysanthemum's autumn bloom, the studio's aftercare
philosophy — ink heals, and the ritual of healing is part of the craft.

## Wholeness
The mandala — the self as a complete universe. Meditative symmetry around the
spine.

## Homecoming
The swallow's return to land. Journeys survived, promises kept.
