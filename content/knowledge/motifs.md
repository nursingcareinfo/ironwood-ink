# Motifs at Ironwood Ink

The visual vocabulary of the studio. Each motif carries layered meaning
(see meanings.md) and belongs to one or more style families (see styles.md).

## Koi
The carp. In Japanese tradition the koi swims upstream against the current —
perseverance and overcoming adversity. A dragon koi has completed its journey
and transformed. Japanese / Irezumi motif.

## Chrysanthemum
The autumn flower of Japan, associated with the emperor and the sun. Represents
perfection, rejuvenation, and the cycle of life. Often paired with koi or dragons.
Japanese / Irezumi motif.

## Dragon
Strength, wisdom, and protection. Japanese dragons are water deities — generous
and protective rather than the fire-breathing beasts of the west. Japanese /
Irezumi and neo-traditional motif.

## Hannya mask
The jealous woman of Noh theater transformed by rage into a demon. Represents
the destructive power of obsession. Neo-traditional and Japanese motif.

## Rose
Love, secrecy, and balance of beauty and pain (thorns). The single most repeated
American Traditional motif. Pairs with daggers (betrayal/love) and swallows.

## Swallow
The returning bird. Traditional sailors tattooed swallows to mark homecoming —
every swallow means a journey survived. Loyalty and return.

## Dagger
Betrayal, sharp truth, or protection. Often paired with a heart or a rose.

## Heart
Love, courage, devotion. In traditional work usually drawn with a banner.

## Skull
Memento mori — remember that you die. Acceptance of mortality, honoring the dead.
Blackwork and traditional motif.

## Serpent
Transformation (shedding skin), danger, healing (the medical caduceus). Blackwork
and neo-traditional motif.

## Tiger
Protection against evil spirits (in Japanese lore), power, unpredictability.

## Monstera leaf
The modern botanical favorite — growth, connection, tropical ease. Fine line motif.

## Mandala
Sacred geometry — the universe, wholeness, meditation. Dotwork motif.

## Hannya and tiger lily
The tiger lily flower traditionally associated with pride and wealth; pairing
with the hannya mask makes a contrast of anger and pride.

## Waves (seigaiha)
Stylized overlapping waves — the sea, calm persistence, good fortune. Japanese
background motif.

## Wind bars
Diagonal bars representing wind — movement and flow behind the main subject in
Japanese compositions.
