# Placements and Anatomy

Placement is a design decision, not an afterthought. The atlas connects
placements to motifs and meanings.

## Forearm
A high-visibility canvas. Fine line botanicals and traditional flash both read
well. Lower pain threshold. Popular first-tattoo placement.

## Upper arm / shoulder
The classic first-piece placement. Large enough for real composition, easy to
cover for work. Sleeves begin here.

## Full sleeve
Shoulder to wrist, designed as one continuous composition. Requires 4–6 sessions.
Japanese backgrounds must flow across the triceps and bicep — the hardest part
of the sleeve.

## Back
The largest canvas. Full back pieces take 6–10 sessions. Traditional home of
Japanese irezumi composition and memento-mori statements.

## Chest
Visible, personal, closer to the heart. Mandalas and hannya masks sit well here.
Sternum work is high-pain, high-reward.

## Ribs
High pain, private placement. Fine line and script work best.

## Leg / thigh
Large surface, lower pain than torso. Good for larger motifs and geometric work.

## Hand / fingers
High visibility, high wear, faster fade. We advise honestly — hands are a
statement, not a starter piece.
